7-week sermon series · Acts 1–8 · Narrative approach

Acts — The Story Continues

The story of Jesus doesn’t end with the Gospels. It keeps going through the Holy Spirit working in ordinary people — including us.

The point, in three lines

Jesus is still at workActs isn’t about heroic apostles or a perfect church. It’s what Jesus keeps doing through ordinary, Spirit-empowered people.
The story keeps getting biggerAgain and again, people learn that God’s work is larger than they assumed. There is more to the story than we thought.
We’re in it, not starring in itWe aren’t watching the story, and we aren’t the main character. Jesus stays at the center; we participate.

We’re not going verse by verse. This is a map, not an itinerary: we follow the story and stop where it turns, expands, or shows us something about Jesus, the Spirit, and God’s people. The goal isn’t to cover Acts. It’s to discover its story together.

The 7 weeks

WEEK1

The Story Continues

Acts 1:1–11
What does it mean to live in the part of the story where Jesus has ascended, the Spirit is coming, and the story is still unfolding?

What happens. Luke ties Acts straight to his Gospel: Jesus’ story isn’t finished. Jesus ascends, but the disciples aren’t handed a story to manage alone. They’re told to wait for the Spirit, then given the direction of the story: Jerusalem → Judea → Samaria → the ends of the earth.

Why it matters. This week is the foundation. It sets the map for everything that follows.

WEEK2

The People We Pass By

Acts 3:1–10
The Spirit sends us toward people we might otherwise pass by.

What happens. Peter and John meet the man at the temple gate. Jesus’ story continues through an encounter with someone who could easily have stayed invisible.

Watch out for. This isn’t a lesson on doing more ministry. We’ll look at what the text says about seeing people, presence, healing, and the continuing work of Jesus.

WEEK3

Telling What We’ve Seen

Acts 3:11–26
Witness begins with telling what we’ve seen God do.

What happens. The miracle becomes Peter’s opening to tell the story of Jesus. What God has done isn’t something he can keep to himself.

Why it matters. Witness here isn’t a technique or a program. It’s participation in the story God is already telling.

WEEK4

Ordinary People

Acts 4:1–22
People who have been with Jesus can become courageous witnesses.

What happens. Peter and John are hauled before the authorities, who notice something about them: ordinary people who have been with Jesus. Their courage doesn’t come from status, education, or institutional power.

Why it matters. A key part of the Acts imagination: God’s work isn’t reserved for professionals or experts.

WEEK5

A People

Acts 4:23–37
What happens when Jesus’ story doesn’t just change individuals, but forms a people together?

What happens. Facing opposition, the church prays together. Then Luke describes their shared life: unity, generosity, worship, and care for one another.

Why it matters. The Spirit isn’t only empowering individuals. God is forming a people. This may become one of the central church-identity passages of the series.

WEEK6

Growing Pains

Acts 6:1–7
Spirit-filled churches still have problems. Faithfulness means learning how to grow together.

What happens. The church has grown, and some widows are being neglected. The apostles don’t try to fix everything personally. The community names the problem, develops leaders, and builds a structure so ministry can continue.

Watch out for. Acts doesn’t present a perfect church; growth creates new challenges. We’ll explore leadership, shared responsibility, and making room for people, without turning the sermon into a lesson on church administration.

WEEK7

The Story Gets Bigger

Acts 8:26–40
What happens when we become attentive to the people and places the Spirit leads us toward?

What happens. The Spirit leads Philip down an unexpected road to an unexpected person. A conversation begins around Scripture, Philip helps the man understand Jesus, and the Ethiopian is baptized.

Why it ends here. The story that began in Jerusalem is now reaching far beyond the boundaries the disciples first imagined. It’s a natural stopping point, not a finish line.

Threads that run through every week

“I am not watching the story. I am in the story.”…and the second half: I am not the main character. Jesus stays at the center.
“There is more to the story than we thought.”Disciples, Jerusalem, Peter, Philip: each finds God’s work is bigger than they assumed.

Why this matters for us, now

God didn’t stop workingThe story didn’t end with a previous generation, with the “old-time religion” we remember, or with an experience we once had with God.
Expect, and be surprisedActs gives us imagination for a church that expects God to keep working, while staying humble enough to let God surprise us about what that looks like.

The larger movement

Jesus continues His work→The Spirit comes→Ordinary people participate→A people is formed→The church learns through faithfulness and failure→The boundaries expand→The story keeps going

We leave this first journey here, not because the story is finished, but because the story continues. Future series can pick up at Acts 9 and beyond.
